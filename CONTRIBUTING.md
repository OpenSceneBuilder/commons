# Contributing to OSB

Start with a question worth answering and a piece of work small enough to finish.

## Your first contribution

1. Read the roadmap and review questions.
2. Open a **Start a contribution** issue. Describe what you want to improve and the time you can reasonably commit.
3. Agree the task and a named reviewer with Rene (@BIM4GIB).
4. Work with public or synthetic examples. Use a branch and pull request for proposed document changes.
5. Explain the result, evidence and remaining questions. Record contributors in the merged work.

For software projects, the maintainer must first supply the license and contribution terms, tested setup instructions and suitable access. An invitation to discuss an idea is not a transfer of rights or a request to publish private source.

## A good task brief

State the problem, relevant context, safe example inputs, expected effort, acceptance evidence and reviewer. Give the contributor room to challenge the premise.

## Reviews

Explain why you recommend a change. Distinguish defects from preferences. Maintainers should agree a review date when taking on a task; no fixed response-time promise is made while the team is forming.

Keep significant decisions in repository records so they remain understandable beyond a chat.

## Access

Contributors receive access to the projects they are working on. Public and private repositories are separate workspaces. Do not copy private discussions, code, logs or models into public issues or pull requests.

Only contribute material you are entitled to share. Use synthetic data for demonstrations unless publication rights are clear.
